Albinism
Chapter 2

How is albinism diagnosed?

Albinism is usually noticed very early — often the moment a baby is born.

First, somebody notices

Newborns with albinism often have very pale skin, white or very light hair, and light-coloured eyes. A nurse or a doctor may spot it during the first check-up.

Sometimes it is less obvious at first, especially if the baby's colouring is similar to the rest of the family's. Then it may be noticed later, when a child keeps holding books close to their face or complains about bright light.

What usually happens, step by step

  1. 1
    A doctor looks closely at the baby's skin, hair and eyes.
  2. 2
    The baby is usually sent to an eye doctor (an ophthalmologist), who looks inside the eyes and checks how well the baby sees.
  3. 3
    Small sticky pads may be placed on the scalp to test how the eyes send signals to the brain. It does not hurt.
  4. 4
    A DNA test can show which type of albinism it is, and which gene is involved.
  5. 5
    Families can talk to a genetic counsellor about what it means for other children they may have.

What the eye doctor looks for

  • Quick side-to-side eye movements that the person cannot control (nystagmus)
  • Eyes that point in different directions (a squint)
  • An iris that is very pale and lets light through
  • Eyes that are sensitive to light
  • The retina — the back of the eye — not developing completely
  • Blurry vision that glasses alone cannot fully fix
Nobody did anything wrong

Albinism is not caused by anything a parent did before or during pregnancy. It is not caused by food, weather, illness or anything in the environment.
